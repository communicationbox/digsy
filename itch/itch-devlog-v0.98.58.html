The version on itch was several months old. This post covers everything since: a second collection to chase, a museum you can walk through, and a long pass over every single thing on screen — because once you start noticing right angles, you can't stop.

Amber: the collection keeps going

Finishing a Museum case used to be the end of that species: every duplicate after that was just coins. Now, once a case is complete, that species starts turning up as amber pieces ✨. Five of them light up an amber case, glowing orange among the gold ones, and it pays well. Rarer species drop amber more easily, so the legendaries don't become a wall.

A museum you walk through

The Museum is no longer a single room with panels on the wall. It's a building with real walls: an entrance hall with the Curator's desk three steps from the door, a rotunda with a mounted skeleton in the middle, and six rooms — one per biome — with their name on a plaque above the cases. Benches to sit on, velvet ropes, a spotlight on every display. Bring the Curator your raw finds, come back the next day, and the new pieces go on display.

Breeding, and an egg that actually hatches

Two creatures from your yard — chimeras or awakened species — lay an egg that takes a couple of days to mature. You pick which parent each part comes from, feed it your least valuable duplicates, and wait. When it's ready, the egg wobbles, cracks spread and branch out, the shell splits in two, and the little one jumps out. It took three seconds to be worth watching, so now it takes three.

The world stopped looking like a grid

This is the part that took the longest, and it's everywhere:

  • Biome borders are a single line shared by both tiles: brown bites into yellow and yellow into brown, in fine 1–3 pixel teeth. No more straight walls of colour, and no more one-tile biomes.
  • Furniture lost its right angles: the barber chair is all curves, the barrels bulge, the alembic is a real glass globe, the dress form is a little wooden figure wearing a dress, curtains fall and fan out, rugs have soft corners.
  • Every sprite has a coloured outline, never black — and there's a test that measures all 428 of them. In this game an outline means "you can do something with this", so scenery has none.
  • Curated variety: four kinds of cactus, four ice crystals, four seashells, four desert rocks — each drawn by hand, with a test that refuses duplicates.
  • All obstacles removed from the open world. What blocks you now is only what you can chop or mine.

Smaller things you'll notice

  • The first letter from Grandpa is hidden behind the plaque of his statue, now standing on its own corner of the square.
  • Skin tone can be changed at the barber, for free — it's who you are, not a haircut.
  • Bigger bags: two new sizes above 40, up to 66 slots, for when you're far from home with amber to carry.
  • Your companion no longer gets stuck on the corner of a house: after half a second it steps back onto the path you actually walked.
  • Shop animals face you now, the barber pole spins seamlessly, and the museum turtle lets you pet it.

Still free, still in the browser, still no combat and no way to lose. If you played an older build, your save carries over.
