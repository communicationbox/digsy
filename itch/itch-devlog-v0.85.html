Most people who opened Digsy World stopped playing within five minutes. I finally sat down and measured why, instead of guessing — and the answer was embarrassing.

You start with zero coins and no spade. Digging is the only verb in the game, and without a spade it answers "you need a spade (Shop)". The spade costs 15 coins, and there is nothing to pick up within ten tiles of where you wake up. So the first few minutes were: try the one thing the game is about, get told no, wander with no idea why.

An opening tutorial, in four objectives

The game now hands you one objective at a time, on a page torn from your grandpa's notebook — the same yellowed paper his letters come on. He is the one who put a shovel in your hands in the intro, so the guidance is his voice, not a system panel.

  • Gather things off the ground — mushrooms, wheat ears, shells. Your only coins until you own a spade.
  • Sell at the Shop and buy the spade
  • Dig
  • Take your find to the Museum

That is the game's whole loop, in about two minutes. An arrow points at what you need — the nearest thing worth picking up, the Shop door, the Museum door — so the opening is a guided hunt instead of a blind sweep. You can skip it whenever you like, and redo it from the Guide.

The buildings tell you what they are

Town squares had six painted signs that meant nothing to a new player. When you need to walk into somewhere, every building now shows what it does above its roof — Shop · sell and buy, Museum · identifies, Inn · sleep — and the one you actually want lights up gold.

The intro went from 16 lines to 5

Almost everyone was skipping it, and skipping it also threw away the parts that mattered: that a raw find has to go to the Museum, that the stuff on the ground is money. Those are not gone — the tutorial teaches them by having you do them. Grandpa now only tells you who he was and why it is your turn.

Fixes

  • Leaving the Lab (or any building) no longer wedges you against the fountain or the park fence.
  • Expired missions really leave your list at day's end — and the game tells you when they do.
  • Building signs no longer shake while you walk.
  • Your character stays in view while you scroll the settings in the creator, instead of scrolling off the top.
  • A stray column of pixels with a tank top and dungarees seen from behind.
  • Snacks cost 20 instead of 15, because 15 is what the spade costs and people were buying one instead of the other.

If you play it

The thing I still cannot see is where people get stuck now. If you give it a go, tell me which objective you were on when you stopped, or when it clicked — one line in the comments is worth more than a hundred silent plays.
